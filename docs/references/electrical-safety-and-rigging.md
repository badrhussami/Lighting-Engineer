# Electrical safety and rigging references

This page is a source directory and hazard-awareness checklist, not an electrical design, lift plan, rigging calculation, or legal interpretation. Requirements depend on the jurisdiction, work classification, equipment, venue, task, and authority having jurisdiction (AHJ).

## Stop-and-escalate conditions

Pause and involve the responsible qualified person/venue authority for exposed conductors, suspected energized parts, overheating or burning odor, repeated protective-device operation, damaged connectors/cables, liquid ingress, uncertain isolation, damaged or overloaded suspension hardware, unverified structural attachment, work at height without an approved plan, unexpected movement, or any situation where people could be exposed to shock, fire, falling objects, or a fall.

Never recommend defeating protective earth/grounding, overcurrent or ground-fault protection, interlocks, guards, fall-protection systems, or venue emergency procedures. Do not give an unqualified user step-by-step instructions for hazardous electrical, lifting, or work-at-height operations.

## Electrical planning and work

For any electrical question, first establish the jurisdiction and whether this is temporary production equipment, a permanent installation, maintenance, or construction work. Separate preliminary load arithmetic from conductor/protection/device selection and installation approval. Exact ratings and installation rules depend on equipment nameplates/manuals, supply, duty, ambient conditions, wiring method, venue rules, and adopted code.

### United States starting points
- [NFPA 70: National Electrical Code](https://www.nfpa.org/en/Codes-and-Standards/Document-information-pages/70) — NFPA's official page for edition/status information. The code's applicability and enforceable edition depend on local adoption and amendments; confirm with the AHJ. Do not treat publication year as automatic local adoption.
- [OSHA 1910 Subpart S: Electrical](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910SubpartS) — U.S. federal general-industry electrical requirements; work classification, state plans, other OSHA provisions, and site rules matter.
- [OSHA electrical standards portal](https://www.osha.gov/electrical/standards) — official navigation to electrical standards and related materials.

### International projects
Start with the applicable national/regional legislation, adopted wiring rules and electrical standards, regulator/AHJ requirements, and venue/project rules. International IEC or ISO publication does not by itself establish local legal adoption. Ask the user for location before stating requirements.

## Rigging, overhead equipment, and work at height

Overhead luminaires, accessories, temporary structures, hoists, attachment points, truss, and supporting structures involve distinct load paths and hazards. A manufacturer's fixture clamp rating does not certify the structure or the complete assembly. A secondary retention device does not make an otherwise unapproved attachment safe. Do not calculate or approve structural capacity without qualified engineering inputs and site-specific review.

### Entertainment technology references
- [ESTA TSP published documents](https://tsp.esta.org/tsp/documents/published_docs.php) — search for current standards and scope, including **ANSI E1.53-2025** (overhead mounting of portable luminaires/accessories) and **ANSI E1.50-1-2025** (temporary display system structures). Verify current revision, exact scope, venue/AHJ requirements, and applicability.
- [ESTA TSP documents portal](https://tsp.esta.org/tsp/documents/index.html) — standards, recommended practices, and informational documents.

### United States fall-protection starting points
- [OSHA fall-protection standards](https://www.osha.gov/fall-protection/standards) — official navigation to applicable general-industry/construction provisions and related interpretations.
- [OSHA 1910.28](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.28) — general-industry duty to have fall and falling-object protection; confirm context and exceptions in the complete regulation.
- OSHA's entertainment-industry interpretation notes that applicable requirements depend on whether the work is general industry or construction. Use the live OSHA sources and qualified safety advice; do not generalize one interpretation to every task.

## Before work is authorized

Confirm the named competent/qualified person, approved venue procedure, isolation/permit and access controls, equipment inspection records, load and attachment documentation, exclusion zones, communications, rescue/emergency plan, environmental conditions, and handover/return-to-service criteria. This is a prompt list only; the site's approved plan and law govern.
