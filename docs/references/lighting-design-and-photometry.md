# Lighting design and photometry

This guide gives a repeatable design workflow and points to primary sources. It intentionally does not prescribe universal illuminance, glare, color, or uniformity targets: those depend on the space, task, audience, jurisdiction, owner criteria, and applicable standard.

## Design workflow

1. **Define the brief:** purpose, occupants/performers, activities, visual tasks, operating scenes, day/night use, audience/camera positions, accessibility needs, maintenance access, energy goals, budget, schedule, and acceptance criteria.
2. **Record the basis:** location and governing authority; geometry and mounting heights; surfaces/material reflectances where known; daylight and ambient conditions; environmental exposure; fixture/control constraints; and applicable owner or venue documents.
3. **Translate intent to criteria:** identify relevant horizontal and vertical illuminance, luminance, uniformity, glare/visual comfort, color appearance/fidelity, temporal light artifacts, emergency/emergency-power interactions, and control behavior. Select criteria from the project brief and authoritative applicable sources; document why each criterion applies.
4. **Select equipment from verified data:** exact luminaire model, optics, photometric file/revision, electrical input, control interface, environmental rating, mounting/accessories, thermal limits, and manufacturer installation/maintenance documentation. Compare application suitability and lifecycle/serviceability, not only lumens or wattage.
5. **Calculate and visualize:** document software/version, geometry, calculation planes/points, reflectances, daylight assumptions, luminaire files, aiming, maintenance assumptions, and scene/control states. Review numeric results alongside views, glare risks, shadows, vertical illumination, sightlines, and operational needs.
6. **Coordinate and validate:** coordinate architecture, electrical, controls, structure/rigging, fire/life safety, AV/camera, and maintenance. Review the design for changed inputs and plausible failure cases. Treat calculations as model results, not measured installed performance.
7. **Commission and hand over:** preserve final design basis, schedules, drawings, control intent, product/manual versions, settings, test records, deviations, training, and maintenance information.

## Interpretation cautions

- An average illuminance number alone does not establish a successful design. Consider task visibility, vertical illumination, luminance distribution, glare, contrast, adaptation, scene variation, color, flicker/temporal effects, daylight, and users' needs.
- A rendered image is illustrative; it does not prove a photometric target is met. A calculation is only as good as its geometry, photometric data, assumptions, and settings. Field measurement has its own instrument, calibration, placement, and operating-state limits.
- Do not use a generic reflectance or maintenance factor without documenting its source and project suitability.
- A manufacturer's beam angle, field angle, output, or color claim is not interchangeable with another metric. Check definitions and test conditions in the data source.
- Do not state that a design complies with a standard unless the exact applicable edition, criteria, complete calculation scope, and responsible review are established.

## Authoritative starting points

### IES (Illuminating Engineering Society)
- [IES Standards](https://ies.org/standards/) — standards program, Lighting Library, tools, and current-standard information.
- [IES individual standards](https://ies.org/individual-standards/) — catalogue and status information; access to full standards may require purchase or subscription.
- [IES Lighting Library](https://ies.org/standards/lighting-library/) — standards collections, including applications, measurement/testing, and photometric data.
- [IES standards cross-reference](https://store.ies.org/ies-standards-cross-reference/) — use to check whether older references have been superseded or mapped to newer publications.

### CIE and ISO/CIE
- [CIE Division 3 publications](https://www.cie.co.at/technical-work/divisions/division3/division-publication) — vision, visual ergonomics, and lighting-related publications; confirm publication status and application.
- [ISO/CIE 8995-1:2025, lighting of work places—Indoor](https://www.cie.co.at/publications/light-and-lighting-lighting-work-places-part-1-indoor-0) — publisher listing for the international indoor-workplace standard. Check national adoption and project applicability; do not assume it governs every project.
- [CIE publications](https://www.cie.co.at/publications) — official publication catalogue.

### Entertainment photometry
- [ESTA Technical Standards Program published documents](https://tsp.esta.org/tsp/documents/published_docs.php) — search the live catalogue for photometry standards such as ANSI E1.25, and check current status and scope.

### LED and solid-state lighting background
- [U.S. Department of Energy Solid-State Lighting](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ssl/solid-state-lighting) — DOE SSL publications and technical background.
- [DOE LED Basics](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ssl/led-basics) — LED technology and color-quality background. Use primary product data and applicable IES/CIE methods for project decisions.

## Useful design record fields

For a calculation package, capture at minimum: project/revision; author/checker; source drawings and date; software and version; calculation date; room/area; luminaire manufacturer/model/optics; photometric-file name/revision; mounting and aiming; surface assumptions; calculation surfaces/planes; scene states; maintenance assumptions; results and criteria sources; unresolved assumptions; and field verification/acceptance plan.
